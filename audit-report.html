How a connection becomes a record

  1. Locate a public source naming the dataset or providing a dataset-specific implementation.
  2. Read the context to distinguish a completed evaluation, an implementation path, documented methodological adaptation, and a citation or mention.
  3. Record the source location, methods, version, limitations, and the evidence for its date.
  4. Group related papers, packages, model cards and repositories under one project. Recheck a changed claim against the owning source.
  5. Count only supported external evaluation, integration and methodological-adaptation relationships as reuse. Keep mention-only entries visible but outside reuse totals.

How to read the growth curve

The curve counts source-dated external reuse projects, once per project. Dates may be an arXiv submission, an artifact commit, or an explicitly dated page update. They are documented appearances, not proven first-use dates. A page updated in July may describe work done earlier. No date is back-calculated from a crawler timestamp. The origin release (Mendeley v1, November 21, 2025) and original article (online February 11, 2026; April issue) appear only as timeline context. They are not external adoption.

Material findings and scoring caveats

  • Bao / Guardian Layer: earlier span-alignment and statistics were corrected. The current record uses v5. Detectability after substitution is a limited proxy, and does not establish end-to-end privacy or clinical utility.
  • MedDeID: character recall and character over-redaction differ from exact entity or query-level metrics. The age-exclusion sensitivity changes what is counted as unwanted redaction.
  • AegisVox: the primary contract-intersection score excludes some ASQ-PHI categories. Full-ontology results must accompany it. The ASQ experiment concerns text detection, not the audio system’s end-to-end privacy.
  • Outis and Inkguard: development exposure and tuning splits matter. External authorship does not make every evaluation an untouched held-out test.
  • HANK and Protostar: implementation/annotation policies differ, and some comparison baselines are quoted rather than rerun. Protostar reports recall in differing contexts; this audit does not merge them into a single score.
  • All projects: no results were independently rerun in this audit. Synthetic benchmark performance does not demonstrate real patient-note performance, HIPAA compliance, certification, clinical safety, endorsement, or production deployment.

Deduplication and exclusions

Bao’s paper, Guardian Layer code, reproducibility site and slides are one project. MedDeID’s paper and associated model/repositories are one project. HANK weight formats and related cards are one family. Outis’s PyPI, GitHub and model artifacts are one project. The Korean dataset’s associated downstream model is not counted as a second ASQ-PHI user.

Creator-owned SafeSearch, the original article/repository/data deposit, AMIA dissemination, secondary news or summaries, and a repository fork without verified downstream use are excluded from external reuse counts. Clinical GLiNER KG and EuroPriv-Bench remain mention-only. The audit found no basis to claim all listed models were trained on ASQ-PHI. Unverified private use is unknown.

Custodian Labs · Guardian Layer

Evaluation use · Custodian Labs · arXiv preprint; no peer-reviewed venue verified

Used a 250-query ASQ-PHI subset to compare downstream detection before and after surrogate replacement, within a seven-benchmark study. This tests detectability of replaced identifiers, a proxy for utility.

Method: Latest v5 ASQ endpoint includes 534 replaced spans. Label-agnostic overlap recall across the detector panel is 91.9% before versus 91.1% after replacement; paired change −0.81 percentage points, 90% document-cluster bootstrap interval −1.37 to −0.26.

  • Only the replaced population is tested; replacement covers 80.9% of ASQ gold identifiers.
  • Gemma 4 31B transformed ASQ run covers 101/250 documents.
  • v2 corrected v1 span alignment and statistics; v1 explanations of truncation and redaction were withdrawn. Use v5 for current numerical claims.
  • Authors are affiliated with the transform's developer. No independent replication performed in this audit.

University of Antwerp / UZA · MedDeID

Evaluation use · University of Antwerp; Antwerp University Hospital · arXiv preprint; no peer-reviewed venue verified

Evaluated a separately trained synthetic-English de-identifier on all 1,051 ASQ-PHI queries as an external portability benchmark, alongside six comparators.

Method: All 2,973 annotations were converted to character offsets; one required apostrophe normalization. Label-agnostic annotation-character recall was 98.90% (95% document-cluster interval 98.59–99.19). Raw non-PII character redaction was 6.21%; an age-exclusion sensitivity yielded 0.89%.

  • English training and testing were synthetic; ASQ results do not establish real-English clinical performance.
  • The age-exclusion sensitivity removes redactions over unannotated numeric ages while retaining the original non-PII denominator; it changes the endpoint.
  • Character recall is not exact entity recall, and character over-redaction is not a hard-negative query false-positive rate.
  • Shared post-processing and differing taxonomies affect comparisons; do not combine scores into a cross-study leaderboard.

Oracle Health AI · AegisVox

Evaluation use · Oracle Health AI · arXiv preprint; no peer-reviewed venue verified

Used all 1,051 ASQ-PHI queries for an independent text-detector evaluation within a long-form speech privacy study. This is a text benchmark use, separate from its 390-conversation audio experiments.

Method: Primary exact typed F1 is 94.4% over 2,147 contract-intersection gold elements. Full-ontology exact typed F1 is 79.9% over 2,973 elements; boundary-tolerant full-ontology F1 is 87.3%. Predictions occur in 48/219 hard-negative queries.

  • The primary endpoint excludes ontology categories outside the frozen detector contract; both primary and full-ontology results must be shown together.
  • Facility/organization geography and literal-year annotation disagreements affect scoring.
  • Text detection scores do not estimate identifier recall in speech or end-to-end audio privacy and cannot be multiplied by replacement realization.
  • This is research evidence, not proof of Oracle product deployment, certification or endorsement.

HANK Safe Harbor

Evaluation use · Hank AI · Public software

Uses all 1,051 ASQ-PHI queries as an external benchmark for a local two-encoder detect-and-replace pipeline.

Method: RoBERTa-large and XLM-R-large union plus structural detectors and consistent fake replacement. Reports recall 0.966 on 832 PHI-positive queries and hard-negative over-redaction 2.3% (5/219).

  • Author-reported results; this audit did not rerun inference.
  • AWS baseline is copied from ASQ-PHI authors, not rerun.
  • ASQ-PHI recall is not interchangeable with character-level recall reported by other projects.
  • Card discloses ages including 90+ are not treated as PHI; this differs from Safe Harbor.
  • One adoption family: do not count weight formats or related model cards as separate users.
  • The Hugging Face card is accessible; its linked GitHub repository returned 404 during this audit, limiting independent harness review.

Outis

Evaluation use · Eric Hauser · Public software

Benchmarks Outis, MedDeID and GLiNER on all 1,051 queries; explicitly labels ASQ-PHI as development evidence.

Method: DeBERTa-v3-base BIOES detector. Policy-aligned, label-agnostic ASQ scores: PHI characters masked 99.94%, non-PHI removed 0.18%, fully masked PHI documents 99.52%. Corpus/prediction hashes and pinned models are documented.

  • Failures were inspected during development, so ASQ-PHI is not an independent test.
  • Headline numbers use policy alignment; exact spans, original gold and adjusted scoring give different results.
  • Benchmarks README says evaluation-only while explicitly acknowledging development exposure.
  • PyPI, GitHub and Hugging Face describe one project, deduplicated here.

Inkguard

Evaluation use · Alize Wajahat / inkguard-app · Public software

Uses ASQ-PHI to develop and evaluate a local document-redaction tool, with explicit odd/even tuning and held-out halves.

Method: Reports 97.1% identifiers removed on the odd half, 96.8% on the even tuning half; 8 of 219 hard negatives changed. Measures software before human review.

  • Only the odd half is described as never used for tuning.
  • Results are author-reported and were not independently rerun.
  • Synthetic query benchmark does not validate real clinical records.
  • Scorer was corrected on 18 October; results depend on its alignment and identifier policy.
  • Evaluation date is reported as 18 October; publicly visible initial repository commit is 20 October 2026.

Korean Step 4 PII candidate dataset

Methodological adaptation · bbanany · Public dataset

Credits ASQ-PHI’s question, record and memo-style variety as inspiration for a 10,000-example Korean PII candidate-classification dataset.

Method: 1,000 synthetic profiles × 10 tags; 8,000 train /1,000 validation /1,000 test. Designed for Qwen supervised tuning after an upstream NER stage; source-reference metadata points to 1,051 ASQ-PHI queries.

  • This documents adaptation of the dataset-construction approach; direct reuse of ASQ-PHI records for training or evaluation was not established.
  • Validation and test share generation frames and phrase components with training; card limits them to in-domain diagnostics.
  • Related Qwen model is downstream of this dataset, not a separate independently verified ASQ-PHI adoption.
